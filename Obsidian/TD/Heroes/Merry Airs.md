*Agent-authored concept, 2026-08-14. Written under delegated design authority; the user's verbatim brief is quoted below.*

She is never quite standing. Catch her at any moment and she is a third of the way
through a turn — up on the ball of one foot, the other toe trailing behind her for
balance, hips gone one way and shoulders the other, and a coat flung out around her
thighs in a hem that sits higher at the back than the front and has not been level in
years. Everything she wears has been whitewashed: linen, wicker, pale ash-wood, dull
tin, all of it limed over and over until the seams have filled in. The one dark thing
on her is the mask — black-glazed earthenware, fired hard and crazed all over like an
old plate, with a short straight beak canted *up* and cocked to one side, and a chip
out of the tip she has never repaired. There are no lenses in it. There is no room:
the whole mask is packed to bursting with dried blooms and straw, and they have come
out through both eye sockets and every breathing slot, a rose-coloured nosegay where a
face should be. High over her head, at the top of a straight arm, she holds a pierced
tin dredger the size of a churn, upended and half a shake into the next one. Her other
hand is behind her, low and out of sight, and hanging from it — mouth-down at her heel,
stopper out — is a small jar quietly emptying into the ground.

She calls it *seasoning*. Everyone she has ever passed remembers how lovely she was
about it, for about a week.

---

## Name

**Merry Airs.** `plaguedoc000` is the code name.

The register is the [[Silent Knight]]'s: two words, a pleasant everyday phrase turned
lethal by the company it keeps. *Silent Night* becomes a knight; *merry airs* — the
cheerful tunes she is plainly dancing to — become the bad air she is actually spreading.
The third meaning is the one that names her personality rather than her trade: she
*puts on airs*. She is performing, constantly, for an audience that is dying.

Runners-up, recorded so they are not re-proposed: *Sweet Ague*, *Merry Malady*,
*Kind Contagion*.

Design authority for lore and personality was delegated in full, exactly as it was for
the [[Good Doctor]]'s first pass. What follows is the brief written against that
delegation, and it is the aim for the current round of work.

### The gap this hero fills

The hard constraint on this design is that the roster **already contains a beaked
plague doctor** and he is staying. Two beaked masks now coexist. Every decision below
exists to make that a family resemblance rather than a duplicate, and the test each
decision had to pass was: *if this would also be true of him, it is the wrong decision.*

She keeps the beak, because the beak is the silhouette the user asked for. She keeps
nothing else of his.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material language | Steel + gold | Silk + silver | Copper, brass, glass, tarred leather | **Whitewash — limed linen, raw wicker, pale ash-wood, dull tin; and black-glazed ceramic** |
| Mass | Upright, broad-shouldered, planted | Upright, tall, narrow, planted | Squat, hunched, back-heavy, leaning | **Turning. Up on the ball of one foot, counter-rotated, wide at the thigh and needle-thin at the ground** |

Four heroes now answer the same question — *hide the face* — four different ways. The
knight took the glow, the mage took the darkness, the Good Doctor took the glass, and
she takes the only remaining answer: there is no opening because there is no space.

Note also the **value** inversion, which is the fastest read of all and the cheapest to
protect: the other three heroes are dark figures carrying a bright accent. She is a
near-white figure whose *mask* is the only dark thing on her. On him the mask
disappears into a black coat. On her it floats.

### The three features that name her

In priority order, silhouette-only, angle-independent. Everything else yields to these.
None of them is a front-view feature; all three survive a full orbit because all three
are true of the whole figure rather than of one face of it.

1. **The turn.** She is mid-rotation — leading foot up on the ball, trailing toe
   touching behind, hips and shoulders counter-rotated hard against each other, coat
   flung out into a rising spiral. She is the only hero not planted and not braced, and
   the only one whose ground contact is two small points instead of a base. From every
   bearing of the orbit the figure is caught in a different part of the same twist,
   which is the point: a counter-rotated body has no repeated view.
2. **The raised dredger.** One arm straight up and over, holding a big pierced tin
   vessel inverted past vertical, mid-shake. Every other hero's signature object hangs
   or is driven into the ground; hers is held *above* the whole silhouette, which puts
   her mass where all three of the others are empty. The tallest point on the model is
   her hand, so her tallest point is an action.
3. **The over-packed beak, tilted up.** Short, straight, canted upward and rolled off
   axis on a head that is itself cocked. His beak is long, down-curved and grim — a
   vulture. Hers points at the sky — a wren, a magpie, something bright-eyed and about
   to steal from you. And it is stuffed to failure: the dried posy that a plague mask
   was historically *for* has been packed in past capacity and is coming back out
   through the eyes and the vents, so the mask's outline is broken all the way round
   and reads as full from any angle.

| Beak | **Short, straight, canted up ~20°, rolled off axis**, on a head cocked to one side | The single cheapest personality inversion available. A down-curve is grim; an up-tilt is pert. Same silhouette family, opposite character |
| Body material | **Whitewash** — limed linen, raw wicker, pale ash-wood, dull tin, chalked until the seams have filled | Copper, brass and tar are his; steel and gold the knight's; silk and silver the mage's. | Value | She is **the pale hero**. Near-white body, black mask, dark only in recesses | The other three are dark grounds with a bright accent. ### Where the mischief is

"Mischievous" is the load-bearing word in the brief, and it has to be geometry. Prose
does not satisfy it. Five specific carriers, in the order they were designed:

1. **She is mid-action, not posed.** The turn is not a stance, it is an instant. A
   figure caught between two positions is doing something; a figure standing is being
   something. Everything else here depends on this reading surviving.
2. **The hidden hand.** An arm cranked behind the back is the most legible mischief
   gesture in body language, and it is the one that makes a 360° orbit into a joke with
   a setup and a punchline: from the front you see an arm going somewhere and a dark
   spot at her heel, and going round the back tells you what it was. A design whose
   payoff *is* the orbit is a design built for the stimulus it will be judged on.
3. **The domestic gesture, misapplied.** She is not casting or striking. She is
   *seasoning* — the raised dredger, the flick of the wrist, the head tilted to watch
   where it lands. The comedy is character, not parody: this is genuinely how she works
   and she is genuinely good at it.
4. **The head cock.** Cheap, tiny, and reads from every bearing. A tilted head over a
   twisted spine is curiosity. Level it and she becomes a statue.
5. **The rose bloom settled on her own shoulders.** She has been doing this for years
   and has never once stood upwind. It is the only detail that is about her rather than
   about her victims.

How this could be got wrong, stated plainly so it is chargeable in review:

- **If the turn reads as combat, it is wrong.** A twist plus a raised arm can easily
  become a wind-up for a strike. She is not winding up. The tell is the trailing toe
  and the wrist: light contact and a loose wrist read as a dance step; a braced rear
  foot and a locked wrist read as a swing.
- **If she reads as menacing rather than delighted, it is wrong.** The whole roster is
  already menacing. She is the one who is *enjoying herself*, and that has to be
  visible with the interior detail gone. Hunching her, lowering the head, or
  down-curving the beak all destroy it in one move.
- **If she reads as a witch, it is wrong.** No pointed hat (the mage's), no cauldron,
  no broom. Everything she carries is kitchen and pantry equipment, deliberately.
- **If the dredger becomes a weapon, it is wrong.** It is a tin, it is dented, it is
  domestic. The moment it acquires a haft, a blade, a spike or a skull it becomes a
  mace and the joke is gone.
- **If the hidden hand is not readable from behind, it has failed entirely.** It is the
  payoff. It must be unambiguous from the rear bearings — jar clearly inverted, stopper
  clearly out, the pool clearly on the ground and clearly beyond her heel.
- **If the emission reads as a beam, it is wrong.** The brief says she spreads to those
  *around* her. Cone, ring, cloud, settling dust, spreading pool — never a line, never
  a jet, never a projectile trail.
- **If she is fully symmetric anywhere, it is wrong.** Both hands doing the same thing,
  a level hem, a level head, a centred mask: each of these individually kills the read.

### Rough scale

| | Metres |
|---|---|
| Crown (dome of the mask) | **~1.78** |
| Beak tip | ~1.70, and forward of the chest |
| Raised hand | ~2.20 |
| **Tallest point** — top of the upended dredger | **~2.36** |
| Widest span (hem, flung out) | ~1.15, centred at ~0.85 height |
| Ground footprint (ball of foot + trailing toe) | ~0.34 across, two contact points |

She is **taller than the Good Doctor** — ~14 cm more at the crown (1.78 against his
1.64) and ~32 cm more at the tallest point (2.36 against his 2.04) — and she gets there
on far less material. He is short and enormously wide; she is tall, slim and only wide
in the middle, and the widest thing about her is cloth in motion rather than equipment
strapped on. She is not the tallest hero; the Mage keeps that.

The ratio that matters more than any single number: **her widest point is roughly three
and a half times her ground footprint.** That inversion — broad in the air, almost
nothing on the floor — is the mass, and any change that thickens the base or narrows
the hem has taken it away.

### Lore

She was not a physician. She was a potter.

Her family fired black-glazed ware in a lime-washed village three days upriver — plates,
jars, chimney pots, the sort of thing bought by people who will never think about who
made it. When the sickness came downriver ahead of any warning, the physicians' guild in
the town needed masks, hundreds of them, faster than any leatherworker could cut them.
So they came upriver and commissioned them in fired clay from the only kiln that could
take the volume, and she threw and glazed every single one herself over four months,
alone, in a shed that never went below blood heat.

Then the guild sealed her village's gates to save the town, and let it burn itself out.
She was outside them at the time, sitting on a cart with the last delivery.

She kept one mask: the one she had thrown wrong, early on, when she was still bored
enough to make jokes in clay — the beak too short, tilted up, cocked to one side, the
tip chipped in the kiln. It was never going to pass. It is the only face she has had
since.

She does not think of herself as spreading anything. She thinks of herself as
**finishing a delivery**. The town owes her for four months' work and she has been
collecting in kind ever since, one household at a time, in no hurry whatsoever, with the
manners of a woman bringing a casserole to a neighbour who has had a hard week. She
knocks. She curtseys. She admires the garden. She is remembered fondly, briefly.

**Her relationship to the [[Good Doctor]] is the best thing about her, and it is
one-sided.**

She made his mask. It came out of her kiln in the third month, one of the good ones,
and she has the number of it. He has never taken it off in front of anyone, so he has
never seen the maker's mark pressed into the inside of the crown, which is a small
five-petalled flower.

He does not know she exists as anything but a rumour. She knows everything about him,
including what he eats, and she follows him — not out of malice; she is genuinely fond
of him. He is the only person in the entire affair who paid on time. She turns up
wherever he is working, three streets behind, whitening what he has fumigated,
seasoning what he has scrubbed, and he has never once caught her at it.

He believes the sickness follows him. He is right about the following and wrong about
the cause. The line in his own file — *he arrived the week after the sickness did, and
nobody has ever established which order those two events belong in* — has an answer, and
the answer is her, and she has never told him, and she has told absolutely everybody
else.

If they ever met properly she would like him very much. She is aware of this and finds
it funny, which is the closest thing to remorse she has.

### Voice

Grim and delighted at once. She is polite, she is never in a hurry, and she never once
says what she is actually doing.

- "Don't get up. I'll only be a moment, and you'll want to be lying down."
- "It wants *salt*. Everything here wants salt. You've all been so careful, and so
  bland."
- "I made his, you know. Third month. Number four hundred and something — I'd stopped
  counting properly by then, and honestly, so had he."
- "Lovely garden. I'll come back when it's had a season."
- "I knock. I always knock. It's not my fault nobody's been answering."
- "Oh, don't thank me. Thank me next week, when you've had time to feel it."

### Known gaps

Deliberately left undecided, so the modelling pipeline resolves them with geometry
rather than this document resolving them with prose:

- **Which foot leads and which way she turns.** Left-handed dredger or right is open.
  The only constraint is that the raised arm and the hidden arm are opposite, and that
  the hem's high side is behind the direction of the turn.
- **How much of the turn.** Anywhere from a quarter-step to a full spin caught at its
  fastest. The correct amount is whatever makes the counter-rotation read from all
  bearings without the model looking like it is falling over.
- **What the dredger is made of, exactly.** Tin is the standing decision; whether it is
  a repurposed flour dredger, a censer she has un-consecrated, or something she threw
  herself is open, and each implies a different perforation pattern.
- **The pool at her heel.** Geometry or a shader effect is undecided, as is whether it
  is a pool, a stain, or dust already settling. It must read as spreading and must sit
  clearly beyond her foot.
- **The dust cone.** Same question as the Good Doctor's unvented chimney, and the two
  should probably be answered together and consistently.
- **Hands.** Blockout mitts are fine to start. Whether she gets digits matters more here
  than on other heroes because both of her hands are *doing* something — the shake and
  the dangle are both wrist reads.
- **Whether anything is written on her.** A lime-washed figure is a surface people write
  on: a chalked cross, a tally, a date. Tempting, historically right, and completely
  undecided. If used it must not become decoration on a coat — that is the mistake this
  project has already paid for twice.
- **No audit script, not rigged, no health check.** Same position the Good Doctor was in
  at blockout. Recorded so it is not mistaken for an oversight.
