The Mage is a figure nobody in the camp can describe twice the same way. He stands a head taller than the men around him, wrapped in a midnight-blue silk robe worn loose — never belted tight, never straightened, always half-slipping as though he had thrown it on a moment before anyone looked. A broad-brimmed wizard's hat throws his whole face into shadow; all that escapes it is a long pale beard spilling down over his chest, so that the only proof there is a man under there at all is the beard and the two hands. In his right he carries the Staff, a gnarled length of blackwood taller than he is, planted point-down in the dirt and never once seen to move. Crowning it, held in a cradle of silver claws, is a single violet gem the size of a fist. The gem is the only thing about him that gives off light, and men who have stood watch beside him swear it is brighter on the nights something is coming.

*Working name. "Mage" is the code name, not the final player-facing name — see Naming below.*

## Brief — 2026-08-01 (third pass)

*The user's words, verbatim. This is the aim for the current round of work.*

> mage, make him look more godly. give him items he wears as apparel which point
> towards an epic previous adventure. Make the robe look grand and pearlescent

Three things to aim at, drawn from that:

1. **Godly, not merely powerful.** The register goes up from "old and dangerous"
   to "something that should not be spoken to casually". Second pass chased
   *dark* — this pass chases *exalted*. Where the two conflict, the darkness
   becomes the contrast that makes the divinity read, not the subject.
2. **Worn apparel that names a past adventure.** Distinct from the second pass's
   "brutal stories in his kit": these are *garments and regalia he wears*, not
   damage and trophies hung off a belt. Each piece should imply one specific,
   completed, epic event — a thing survived and taken, worn since.
3. **The robe grand and pearlescent.** The "void and starlight" palette below
   already made the robe angle-dependent and iridescent, deliberately keeping
   every base value *down*. "Grand and pearlescent" pushes against that. The
   tension is real and is the interesting part of this pass: find grandeur that
   does not spend the contrast the gem needs.

### Carried forward from the second pass

Five things to aim at, drawn from that:

1. **A dark aura from the soul** — darkness as an emission, not merely as unlit
   surface. Whatever form it takes it has to survive the 184 icon.
2. **A thousand years of it** — age carried by the *objects*, not by a stooped
   pose. He is still upright.
3. **Brutal stories in his kit** — every item he carries should imply an event.
   Trophies, damage, repairs, things taken. The belt kit and the staff are the
   obvious carriers; the robe's tears are fair game.
4. **The gem is the most complexly beautiful object in the realms** — it is
   allowed to be the single most detailed thing on the model. Everything else
   yields contrast to it.
5. **A friendly beard against a mysterious void** — the warm/ominous split in the
   head is deliberate and should be *widened*, not resolved. This is the
   grim/silly clash the project tone asks for, expressed inside one character.

## Brief — 2026-08-03 (fifth pass)

*The user's words, verbatim.*

> more aura

The shortest brief yet, and it is a **return to the second pass's first aim** —
"a dark aura emanating from his soul" — rather than a new direction. Read it as:
the aura is currently under-delivered and should be pushed much further.

What "aura" has to mean here, given the constraints already measured:

1. **Aura is emitted, not painted.** The second pass already settled that the
   darkness is an *emission*, not merely unlit surface. An aura that is just a
   darker patch of robe is not an aura; it has to read as something the figure
   is giving off — light, dark-light, displaced air, drifting matter.
2. **It must survive the silhouette.** The standing evidence is that silhouette
   changes are what survive; a purely shader-side glow that vanishes at small
   sizes has failed. Geometry that carries the aura — motes, tatters, rising
   shards, ground effect — is fair game and probably necessary.
3. **It must not spend the gem's contrast.** The gem remains the brightest, most
   complex object on the model. An aura bright enough to compete with it has
   broken a settled decision. The natural resolution is that the aura is *dark*
   emission and the gem is the only *bright* one — they occupy opposite ends.
4. **It comes off him, so it should imply where from.** "From his soul" locates
   it at the body, not at the staff. The void under the brim and the torso are
   the sources with the most standing evidence behind them.

Everything in the third and fourth passes still stands; this narrows where the
next round of designs should aim, it does not replace them.

### Settled 2026-08-03 — the coat is loosened, the kit stays

An absolute review of the whole 8-view orbit found the model had drifted into the
**exact opposite** of the paragraph at the top of this file. Against "never
belted, always half-slipping as though thrown on a moment before anyone looked",
the render shows an unambiguous belt with a round silver oval buckle, a pouch and
a scroll case slung either side, a full horizontal strap across the small of the
back, a placket buttoned throat to hem, symmetric lapels, a level collar, a V yoke
and puffed seamed sleeve heads — *"a tailored military greatcoat with a waist
cinch, not a robe thrown on a moment ago."*

Five passes of relative (BEFORE/AFTER) judging never caught this, because a
comparator only looks where the last change was, and nothing had ever changed the
belt. **This is why the absolute review runs on every brief change, not only at
finish.**

**The user's decision: loosen it, keep the kit.** Break the tailoring without
deleting anything —

- collar knocked askew, lapels asymmetric;
- placket unbuttoned over part of its run, not fastened throat to hem;
- the belt re-read as a **slung sash, low and off-level**, not a fitted cinch;
- pouch, scroll case and buckle **retained** — they carry the "epic past
  adventure" brief and earlier panels praised them.

A silhouette-and-asymmetry change, not a subtraction. Note the **rear strap does
as much of the tailored work as the front belt**: loosening only the front still
reads cinched from behind.

The same review rated the aura `absent`: *"nothing in any of the eight views reads

The target, in its words, and the clearest statement of the aura brief anyone has

### Overturned 2026-08-12 — the brim/beard trade is opened

*The user's decision, taken on measured geometry. This supersedes the mid-chest beard
cap in **Appearance** and the refusal to shrink the brim recorded in **Head height**.*

A maker instrumented the occlusion directly — a private elevation ladder at dead-front
azimuth, rig held still, elevation walked 5→72°, counting visible beard pixels — and
solved the brim's real geometry from two measured vanish points: **effective front edge
z = 1.662, horizontal clearance R − r = 0.473 m.**

What that costs, at the doc's mid-chest length:

| To stay visible at | The beard must reach |
|---|---|
| 42.5° | z 1.229 — mid-chest, the current cap |
| 50° | z 1.099 — below `Z_WAIST` (1.145) |
| 60° | z 0.844 — mid-thigh |
| 70° | z 0.364 — ankle |

Above ~50° the beard contributes **zero pixels in every variant**. Six independent blind
reviewers had already reported this unprompted across three waves and two unrelated
designs — *"a mushroom or a manta ray on a pole"* — and it was masking other designs'
improvements badly enough that one reviewer called two visibly different renders
"the identical model".

Both available fixes were previously refused by this document. The user has opened all
three avenues, to be built as **separate designs and judged against each other**:

1. **The brim's underside may be lifted / its droop reduced.** Already named in this doc
   as the upstream fix, and it costs nothing from "broad-brimmed".
2. **The mid-chest beard cap is lifted.** It may run to the waist or the thigh.
3. **The brim's radius may shrink.** "Broad-brimmed" is no longer inviolable.

**Still protected:** the brim's *unbroken swept outline* — d47 was killed for notching it.
Shrinking the radius is not notching it.

The user explicitly declined the fourth option offered, which was to hold this document
and stop counting the defect against designs that did not cause it. The defect stays
chargeable.

Two findings to carry, both measured rather than asserted: the beard lies **entirely
inside the figure's outline** (190 mm of added length moves silhouette IoU by 0.0011), so
it can only ever be read by tone and no silhouette metric will detect a change to it; and
from the two **back** bearings the beard is absent at any elevation, because it is on his
front — "vanishes in three of six views" is partly geometry, not defect.

## Appearance

*Approved 2026-07-31. This section is the source of truth for the model — if the
build script and this section disagree, the script is wrong.*

The three features that have to name the character, in priority order:

1. **The hat.** Broad-brimmed and tall, with the point arcing backward and
   **curling down** — slouched and worn-in, not a costume cone. Chosen
   2026-08-01 from three rendered options. It is the first thing read at any
   size and it is what casts the face into shadow. Note the curl only reads
   off-axis; a head-on render alone will not show it.
2. **The robe.** Midnight-blue silk, worn *loosely* — open down the front over a
   near-black under-robe, wide bell sleeves, hem flaring and pooling on the ground.
   Silk, not wool: many fine folds rather than a few heavy ones.
3. **The staff.** Gnarled blackwood, planted point-down, standing taller than he
   does, with a **large violet gem** crested at the top in a silver claw setting.
   The gem sits *above* the hat so it clears the silhouette.

Supporting decisions:

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Face | Hidden. Dark void under the brim, no visible features | "Mysterious." Rhymes with the [[Silent Knight]]'s hidden face without borrowing his red |
| Beard | Long, pale, spilling from the shadow to mid-chest, built as **five separate forked locks** | Carries the wizard read at icon size, which a shadowed void alone cannot. One lofted mass reads as a bib from the front and a hood at 3/4 — hair needs a broken silhouette edge, and carving ridges into a single surface does not survive the subsurf |
| Build / stance | Tall, upright, staff planted beside him | He is a static hero; this is the pose players stare at all match. Mirrors the knight's planted sword |
| Robe | Midnight blue, silk | — |
| Trim | **Silver**, outlining hem, cuffs, front opening, hat band, shoulder mantle | Modelling rule 3 — trim is what keeps the internal lines alive at small sizes |
| Gem / glow | **Violet** | Must never be confused with the knight's red at a glance in a busy fight. Every VFX this hero ever gets inherits this violet |
| Recesses | Near-black: face void, robe opening, sleeve interiors, under the brim | Modelling rule 4 — the gem needs something to glow against |

**Silhouette test:** at 184×184 with every interior detail lost, the outline must
read as *pointed hat + flared robe + tall staff with a gem on top*. That is the
standing test; `_mage000/icon_184.png` is where it gets checked.

### Head height

The head, neck, beard and hat sit **93 mm higher than a normal figure would put
them**, decided 2026-08-01. This is load-bearing, not a stylistic tweak: with the
head at its anatomical height the brim's outer edge drooped to within 15 cm of
the shoulder line and the hat read as a table resting on his shoulders. The gap
of visible neck between brim and shoulder is what fixes it. Shrinking the brim
also fixes it, but costs "broad-brimmed", which is in the brief.

> **REGRESSED — measured 2026-08-12.** This fix no longer holds. A maker lifted the
> brim's generator out of the script by AST slice and ran it in system Python: the
> lowest brim edge now sits **155 mm above the shoulder line**, against the 150 mm
> that defined the original failure. The 93 mm was eaten by work accepted since —
> **d24** took `BRIM_TILT` to (52, 40, 26, 46) and `BRIM_SAG` to 68°. Nine independent
> blind reviewers across four waves were describing a *measurable regression*, not a
> matter of taste. Relative BEFORE/AFTER judging cannot catch this class of drift,
> because each comparator only looks where the last change was.
>
> **The obvious lever runs backwards.** Reducing the fold angle makes the brim hang
> *lower*: `drop = (R − knee)·(1 − cos φ)/φ`, and while the shape factor peaks near
> φ≈133° (where this brim sits), the knee slides inboard as the fold softens and
> lengthens the hanging cloth — 90° gives drop/R 0.2364 against 133°'s 0.2029. So
> `BRIM_TILT` and `BRIM_SAG` are **anti-levers on edge height**, the same class as
> roughness-on-metal. The lever that is not an anti-lever is the **knee**.

### Structure (rebuilt 2026-08-01)

Five things about how this model is put together are load-bearing, and undoing
any of them brings back a defect that took a close-up render to find:

- **The body cross-section is elliptical, not circular** (`BODY_DEPTH`). A torso
  as deep as it is wide has no shoulder — it is a cone, and the arms read as
  tubes rammed into its side. This is the fix for "the shoulders don't look
  right", not anything applied to the shoulders themselves.
- **The shoulder mantle is gone.** Its hard hem ran horizontally through exactly
  the height where the sleeves flare and sliced visibly through both of them. A
  piped yoke seam on the robe's own surface does the job it was meant to do.
- **The staff stands well out from the body.** Not stylistic: the hand is *on*
  the staff and the sleeve is a tube around the forearm, so a wide bell always
  swallows the shaft. Either the sleeve shrinks to nothing or the staff moves
  out. It moved out.
- **The two sleeves are different.** His left is a full bell; his right is short
  and barely flared, pushed back to grip the staff. This is what makes the
  clearance above work, and it agrees with the lore.
- **The gem's cut is crown-heavy, not a jeweller's brilliant.** A real brilliant
  is mostly pavilion because it is viewed from above through the table. This one
  is seen from the side at the top of a staff, so a correct cut renders as a
  small purple cap sitting on a lot of silver.

Complexity added in the same pass: three ridges winding up the shaft, a wound
leather grip cord, five wooden tendrils holding the stone in a silver seat,
hands with knuckled digits and a real palm, a hem border band, lapel studs, a
sash buckle, and a crease in the hat crown.

**Character:** the hat is cocked 7.5°, the sleeves are asymmetric, and the
fastenings run down one lapel only — all three lean on the same line of lore,
that nothing about him is ever straightened.

### Settled 2026-08-01

- **Folds.** Cusped at power 0.32 with a second finer harmonic at a different
  pitch and drift. A single frequency read as machine pleating; the shallow
  power read as shading rather than cloth.
- **Belt kit.** A leather pouch on the right hip and a rolled scroll on the left,
  both hung from straps that overlap the sash. The waist was the emptiest band
  of the silhouette.
- **Eye glints: rejected, on evidence.** Built, rendered on and off, and the two
  images are indistinguishable at every camera including the 184 icon. **There is
  no visible face from any angle**: the brim's outer edge droops to z 1.741 and
  the beard's top ring starts at 1.815, so between them they occlude the whole
  front of the head from any horizontal or downward view — and the game camera
  looks down. The code is kept behind `EYES_ON = False` purely to record this.
  They were also never floaters; each slit seats into the head sphere, so the
  `FLOATERS_OK` entry originally expected turned out to be unnecessary.

  *If a visible face is ever wanted the fix is upstream — lift the brim's
  underside or start the beard lower — not this flag.*

### Palette — "void and starlight" (2026-08-01)

Chosen from four rendered directions: the previous palette, a *moonlit pearl*
(pale elven driftwood staff, platinum trim), this one, and a *godsilk and
heartwood* (rose-gold silk, warm polished heartwood). The brief was mystic and
mysterious, robes fit for a god, silver as the most precious material known,
and a staff cut from an immortal tree.

The load-bearing idea: **richness comes from angle-dependent colour, not from
brightness.** Every base value in this palette went *down*, not up. That is what
lets the robe be opulent without spending the one thing the figure cannot spare,
which is contrast against the gem — the failure the earlier notes on `C_HAIR`
and `C_SKIN` both record. The two rejected directions each put a light staff
next to the gem; this one is the only one that leaves the gem unchallenged.

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Robe | Base colour is a **three-stop fresnel ramp**, not a value: near-black `0.008,0.011,0.046` facing camera → violet mid → cyan-teal `0.175,0.310,0.470` at grazing. Three stops because a straight blend from near-black to cyan passes through muddy slate; the mid stop keeps the transition through violet. Ramp speed `PEARL_BLEND = 0.30` — higher and the whole robe lifts and the dark base is pointless |
| Iridescence | **Thin film**, 430 nm at IOR 1.92, over the ramp. The ramp shifts the surface hue with angle; thin film shifts the *highlight's* hue too, which is what stops it reading as a two-tone gradient. ~550 nm swings green and fights the gem |
| Silk fibre | Sheen 0.38 with a **blue-tinted** sheen colour. Tinting the fibre highlight is most of why it reads as moonlit rather than merely shiny |
| Lining | Unchanged in role — matt, near-black, roughness 0.78. Rule 4. A second shimmering silk in the robe's opening would take away what the gem glows against |
| Silver | Cold mirror: base 0.925, **roughness 0.062**, anisotropic 0.55. At this roughness it is mostly reflecting the dark world rather than generating its own value, which is what makes it read as precious metal instead of grey paint |
| Staff | Blackwood **stays black**; the life shows as violet veins *in* the grain — noise through a ramp with the stops pinched 0.62→0.78, so only filaments glow. Widening that gap turns the shaft into a fluorescent tube. The five tendrils cradling the stone run hotter (1.20 vs 0.85): the light concentrates where it comes out |
| Gem | Deeper and glassier — roughness 0.032, transmission 0.68, coat 0.60. `GEM_EMIT` untouched, for all the reasons already recorded |
| Beard / skin | Both nudged *down* again, for the reason already in the doc |

**Export caveat.** The shimmer and the vein glow are node graphs and do **not**
survive the FBX — FBX carries one flat colour per material. `mat.diffuse_color`
is set to the ramp's mid stop, so that single value is what Unity imports.
Reproducing this in-engine is a shader job for whoever builds the prefab, and it
should be treated as part of that work rather than as a defect in the export.
